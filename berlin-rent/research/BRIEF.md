# BRIEF: "Why can't anyone in Berlin find a flat?"

Backing doc for the builder. Merged from notes A–D (2026-10-08). Use the full notes for raw tables and extra URLs; this file is the canonical list of what to say. All rents are € net cold (nettokalt) per m² per month unless marked otherwise. "my calc" means the number was derived by a research agent or by me, not printed by the source.

**Corrections that override the original brief:**
- The Mietpreisbremse did NOT expire in 2025. It was extended to 31 Dec 2029, and Berlin's ordinance runs from 1 Jan 2026 to 31 Dec 2029.
- Mietspiegel 2023 was the index update; 2024 was newly surveyed and qualified. A new qualified **Mietspiegel 2026 (€7.71)** came out on 28 May 2026.
- Official population is **3.70M** (Fortschreibung). 3.9M is the residents' register. Always name which one you use.
- Vacancy is about 2%, not "below 1%".
- For DWE, use **57.6% of voters**.
- The Mietendeckel stage-2 rent cuts started 23 Nov 2020; the freeze started 23 Feb 2020.
- The GSW deal was €405M plus €1.56bn of debt (not €2.1bn).
- DWE's second referendum: signature collection has **not** started.
- "Mietrecht II" is a bill in committee, **not law**.

## 1. The 4 hook stats

1. **+119%.** The median asking rent of €15.78 (2025) is more than double the legal local comparative rent of €7.21, "the highest gap among German metros."
   - IBB press release, 2026-03-06: https://www.ibb.de/de/ueber-uns/presse/ibb-wohnungsmarktbericht-2025-stagnierende-angebotsmieten-moderate-bestandsmieten-und-ansehnliche-neubaubilanz.html
   - Footnote: against the new Mietspiegel 2026 (€7.71) the gap is about +105% (my calc).
2. **4.5% / 22 years.** Only 4.5% of flats at BBU member landlords changed hands in 2025, the lowest since records began in 2001. The average tenancy is now more than 22 years, over double 2008.
   - BBU, 2026-09-14: https://bbu.de/beitraege/bbu-jahresstatistik-2025-berlin-fluktuation-bei-den-bbu-mitgliedsunternehmen-berlin-im-jahr-2025
3. **~300 requests per flat, then a lottery.** HOWOGE (a state-owned landlord) gets "on average 300 online requests per flat" and draws 10 people by lot for the viewing.
   - Morgenpost, 2022-08-27 (mirror): https://www.ghb-online.de/oeffentlichkeitsarbeit/pressestimmen/1140-3000-bewerber-fuer-111-wohnungen.html

4. **1,035,950 → 0.** That many Berliners voted Yes to socialise big landlords (2021). Five years later, zero flats have been socialised.
   - Votes: https://www.wahlen-berlin.de/abstimmungen/ve2021/AFSPRAES/ergebnisse.html
   - Five-year score: entwicklungsstadt, 2026-09-12, https://www.entwicklungsstadt.de/deutsche-wohnen-enteignen-was-aus-dem-berliner-volksentscheid-wurde/

## 2. The 3 title questions

**Why is it so expensive?** Demand kept coming and building didn't keep up.
- Population: about 3.33M (2011, Zensus-2011 basis) → 3.70M (2025), roughly +375k (AfS).
- Completions peaked at 18,999 in 2019, never reached the 20k target, and fell to 11,027 in 2025.
- Vacancy is about 2%; a healthy market needs about 3%.
- So the few flats that do come up get priced at the margin: €7.20 (2012) → €15.78 (2025) median asking rent (IBB). That is the "flow" price.
- The "stock" is still cheap: the Zensus 2022 average is €7.67, the cheapest of Germany's big cities.

**Why does nobody move?** Moving doubles your rent.
- A sitting tenant's rent can rise at most 15% in 3 years, and only up to the Mietspiegel (avg €7.71). The next flat costs about €15.78.
- So a 70 m² flat costs about €540 if you stay and about €1,105 if you move (my calc).
- Result: BBU turnover fell from 9.5% (2001) to 4.5% (2025). Fewer moves mean fewer listings, which means even higher asking rents: a feedback loop. IBB itself says the gap "can further dampen turnover."

**Why didn't anything the city tried work?**
- The Mietendeckel worked on paper (asking rents −7 to −11%), but listings halved, and Karlsruhe voided it because Berlin lacked the competence to pass it.
- The Mietpreisbremse is riddled with exemptions: new builds after Oct 2014, the previous tenant's rent, modernisation, and "temporary" furnished lets. There is no fine, and more than 45% of ads probably breach it.
- DWE won 57.6% and socialised 0 flats.
- Meanwhile Berlin had sold GSW for about €30k per flat (incl. debt) and bought flats back at about €167k each.

## 3. The mechanism chain

1. **Berlin was emptying out, so it sold and demolished.**
   - Vacancy was 8.5% (155,000 flats) in 1998 (KA 15/372, 2002, https://pardok.parlament-berlin.de/starweb/adis/citat/VT/15/KlAnfr/ka15-0372.pdf).
   - Around 2001 the Senate planned demolitions (MieterEcho 292/2001, https://www.bmgev.de/mieterecho/292/themen/07.htm).
   - Public stock went from 482,000 (1990) to 273,000 (2005) (Holm/BMGEV; junge Welt 2006-08-05).
2. **Then people came.**
   - Official count: 3,326,002 (2011) → 3,700,577 (2025) (AfS 2026-06-22, https://www.statistik-berlin-brandenburg.de/presse/2026/73-bevoelkerungsfortschreibung-2025-berlin/).
   - 2025 growth came entirely from migration; deaths exceeded births.
3. **Building lagged.**
   - Completions (AfS, https://www.statistik-berlin-brandenburg.de/wirtschaft/wirtschaftsbereiche/gebaeude-und-wohnungen/): 2015 10,722 → 2019 18,999 (peak) → 2025 11,027 (−28%).
   - StEP 2040 target: 100,000 flats in 2022–2026. Actual 2022–25: 59,664 (my sum). StEP: https://www.berlin.de/sen/stadtentwicklung/planung/stadtentwicklungsplaene/step-wohnen-2040/
4. **Building got expensive.**
   - Mortgage rates (>10y fixation) went from 1.27% (Sep 2021) to 3.92% (Nov 2023) and 4.04% (Aug 2026) (Bundesbank s510athyp).
   - Construction prices: index 2021=100 → 133.9 in 2025 (Destatis 61261-0001, Stand 2026-09-21).
   - Flat permits fell to 9,772 in 2024, the lowest since 2012, then 13,754 in 2025 (AfS 2026, https://www.statistik-berlin-brandenburg.de/presse/2026/50-baugenehmigungen-2025-berlin/).
5. **Vacancy hit the floor.**
   - Zensus 2022: 1.97% (40,681 flats). Only 1.2% had been empty for 3 months or more (Drs. 19/21184, https://pardok.parlament-berlin.de/starweb/adis/citat/VT/19/SchrAnfr/S19-21184.pdf).
   - A healthy reserve is about 3% (StEP).
6. **Asking rents ran away from existing rents.**
   - Asking rent: €7.20 → €15.78, 2012–2025 (IBB).
   - Mietspiegel average: €5.84 (2015) → €7.71 (2026). Since 2020 it has been built from rents of the last 6 years, so it lags by design (BGBl. 2019 I S. 2911).
   - Caveat: signed new contracts at BBU members average **€9.54**, not €15.78 (IBB 2026-03-06). Cheap state and co-op flats are often let offline.
7. **The gap locks people in, so turnover collapses.**
   - BBU turnover: 9.5% (2001) → 4.5% (2025).
   - State landlords (LWU): 3.8% in 2024, "formerly around 10%" (Senate Bericht 2024, https://www.berlin.de/sen/bauen/_assets/neubau/bericht_2024_geaendert.pdf).
   - LWU spokesman Malter: 10% turnover "would be 160,000 flats a year… we now get perhaps 60,000" (Morgenpost 2026-04-15).
8. **Low turnover means a tiny market.**
   - About 85–90k new leases per year: ⅓ unfurnished on portals, ⅓ furnished-temporary, ⅓ offline (Drs. 19/26285, 2026-06-23, https://pardok.parlament-berlin.de/starweb/adis/citat/VT/19/SchrAnfr/S19-26285.pdf).
   - Only 2.3% of the stock was advertised on major portals in H1 2024 (JLL via Handelsblatt, 2024-08-23).
9. **Loopholes push supply into furnished-temporary lets.**
   - Under § 549(2) BGB, "temporary use" is exempt from the brake and has no time limit.
   - No statute sets the furniture surcharge.
   - Furnished-temporary (MWZ) share of listings: 13% (2012) → 57% (2023) → 48% (2025). MWZ average €24.14/m² all-in (Drs. 19/26285).
   - Caveat: an Oxford Economics study found no significant causal effect of the brake on furnished listings.
10. **Everyone else queues.** The queue is quantified in section 6.

## 4. The fights, with verdicts

**A. Mietendeckel vs Karlsruhe**
- **Setup:** the Abgeordnetenhaus passed it 85–64–1 on 30 Jan 2020.
  - From 23 Feb 2020: rents frozen at their 18 Jun 2019 level, caps on new lettings, and buildings first occupied from 2014 exempt.
  - From 23 Nov 2020: rents more than 20% over the cap had to be cut (Senate estimate 340k flats; F+B ~512k).
  - About 1.5M flats were frozen.
- **Punch:** asking rents fell 7–11%, but listings roughly halved and Potsdam rents rose ~12% (DIW WB 8/2021, https://www.diw.de/documents/publikationen/73/diw_01.c.811443.de/21-8-3.pdf).
  - IW: listings −51.8%, contacts per listing +67% (IW-Trends 3/2021).
  - Landlords wrote "shadow rents" into contracts in case the law fell.
- **Verdict (exact):** BVerfG 2 BvF 1/20, decided 25 Mar 2021, published 15 Apr 2021. The law is "incompatible with the Basic Law and thus void." The reason is **competence only**: the Bund regulated rent law exhaustively in §§ 556–561 BGB. Decided by Beschluss without oral hearing, on a challenge by 284 CDU/CSU+FDP MPs. Void from the start, so tenants owed back-rent; Vonovia, Heimstaden and all six state firms waived it.
  - https://www.bundesverfassungsgericht.de/SharedDocs/Pressemitteilungen/EN/2021/bvg21-028.html
- **Don't say:** "ruled unconstitutional because rent caps are wrong."

**B. DWE referendum vs outcome (as of 8 Oct 2026)**
- **Setup:** 26 Sep 2021. Yes 1,035,950 = **57.6% of participants** (59.1% of valid votes); turnout 73.5%. It was a non-binding resolution.
  - Target: private landlords with more than 3,000 flats, about 243k flats. Senate compensation estimate €28.8–36bn.
- **Punch:** the next morning (Mon 27 Sep) Vonovia announced it had 50.49% of Deutsche Wohnen (Reuters 2021-09-27).
- **What happened next:**
  - Expert commission, 28 Jun 2023: socialisation is constitutional, Berlin is competent, and compensation may be below market value (9 votes, 3 dissenting, 1 partly agreeing).
  - CDU/SPD passed a Vergesellschaftungsrahmengesetz on 12 Mar 2026. It takes effect about 28 Mar 2028, socialises nothing by itself, and its promised Karlsruhe review had not been filed as of Sep 2026.
  - Federal coalition, 1–2 Jul 2026: a plan to ban state-level socialisation by federal law. **Not passed.**
  - DWE's own draft law (26 Sep 2025): a vote in 2027 at the earliest; signature collection not started.
  - Berlin election, 20 Sep 2026 (preliminary): Linke 25.7%, first place. Linke/Grüne/SPD start exploratory talks on **8 Oct 2026**, with socialisation one of the open points.
- **Verdict wording:** "Legally possible, politically popular, and as of October 2026 not a single flat socialised." Sources: entwicklungsstadt 2026-09-12; tagesschau 2026-10-08 https://www.tagesschau.de/inland/innenpolitik/berlin-sondierungsgespraeche-100.html

**C. Mietpreisbremse vs loopholes**
- **Setup:** Berlin was the first state to use it, from 1 Jun 2015. A new tenancy's rent may be at most Mietspiegel +10%. Extended federally to 2029: Bundestag 26 Jun 2025, in force 23 Jul 2025 (https://www.bundestag.de/dokumente/textarchiv/2025/kw26-de-mietpreisbremse-1084786). Berlin ordinance runs 1 Jan 2026–31 Dec 2029.
- **Punch: exemptions** (§§ 556e–f, 549 BGB, https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__556f.html):
  - flats first let after 1 Oct 2014;
  - first let after comprehensive modernisation;
  - the previous tenant's (higher) rent;
  - "temporary use" with no time limit;
  - furnished surcharge undefined in statute.
  - The BGB has **no fine**.
  - DMB Mietenmonitor (Dec 2025, advocacy): more than 45% of existing-stock ads likely breach it; furnished ads 69% (https://mieterbund.de/app/uploads/2025/12/FactSheet_DMB_Mietenmonitor_2025.pdf).
  - Justice Minister Hubig, 27 Jun 2025: "It can't be that a landlord puts two chairs in an empty flat and thinks he can then charge much higher prices." (BT-Drs. 21/1446)
- **Verdict:** "Alive until 2029, binding on paper, enforced only if *you* complain." Since 2020, a Rüge within 30 months recovers overpaid rent back to day one (§ 556g(2)). The fix, Mietrecht II (BT-Drs. 21/6807), would set a 6–8-month cap on temporary lets and a furnished surcharge of at most 1% of furniture value per month or a presumed 10%. It has been in committee since 9 Jul 2026 and is **not law**.

**D. GSW sale vs buy-back**
- **Setup:** On 25 May 2004 the SPD/PDS Senate (Sarrazin) sold GSW, about 65,700 units, to Cerberus/Whitehall for **€405M plus €1.56bn of debt**. Sarrazin called it a "beachtlicher Erfolg" (a remarkable success). All parties agreed (BBU 2004-05-26, https://bbu.de/beitraege/senat-stimmt-verkauf-der-gsw-zu).
- **Punch:**
  - GSW went to Deutsche Wohnen in 2013, and DW to Vonovia in 2021.
  - On 17 Sep 2021 the state bought about 14,750 flats + 450 commercial units from Vonovia/DW for **€2.46bn** (Senate PR, https://www.berlin.de/sen/finanzen/presse/pressemitteilungen/pressemitteilung.1127587.php), and the CEO said the 1970s/80s buildings contain asbestos.
  - ADO → Gewobag, 2019: about 5,800 flats for €920M.
- **Verdict (per flat, nominal, my calc, different portfolios):** sold at about **€6,200** (cash) or **€29,900** (incl. debt); bought back at about **€166,800**, about 5.6× the 2004 value including debt. Always say "not the same flats, not inflation-adjusted."

**Cheat sheet (8 verified rules)**
1. Deposit: max 3× **net cold**, payable in 3 instalments (§551 BGB).
2. Whoever hires the agent pays (since 2015-06-01, §2(1a) WoVermittG).
3. New-lease rent: max Mietspiegel +10%. Exceptions must be disclosed **before** signing (§556d, §556g(1a)). Free check: Mietpreisprüfstelle, (030) 213 007 302.
4. Sign first, complain later: a written Rüge within 30 months recovers overpaid rent from day one (§556g(2), contracts from 1 Apr 2020).
5. At a viewing: name and address only; no ID photocopy; no questions about nationality, job tenure or tenants'-union membership (DSK Jan 2026). This is regulator guidance, not statute.
6. You don't need a Mietschuldenfreiheitsbescheinigung: the old landlord need not issue one (BGH VIII ZR 238/08), and DSK says landlords can't demand it.
7. Anmeldung within 2 weeks (§17 BMG). In Berlin, a booked appointment counts as meeting the deadline. Online registration is **not** available to newly arrived non-EU citizens.
8. Notice: tenant 3 months. Landlord 3/6/9 months and needs a reason such as Eigenbedarf (§573, §573c BGB).
- Reserve: rigid-interval or unrenovated-handover repaint clauses are often void (BGH 18.03.2015).

**Misconceptions → reality:** "rent is rent" (cold vs warm); "you always pay the Makler"; "furnished is exempt from the brake"; "you can't be evicted"; "1-month notice"; "Anmeldung is optional"; "the brake expired in 2025"; "Mietspiegel = what everyone pays" (it covers only rents changed in the last 6 years). Sources: D §8; B §2.

## 8. Shaky ground (how to phrase safely)

- **Furnished share.** 48% (2025) / 57% (2023 peak) is IBB/Senate *listing* share; furnished flats are re-listed several times a year. vdp: "knapp 25%" peak (June 2025). DMB: 21.8%. Senate flow estimate: about ⅓ of new leases. Say: "up to half of *listings* (about a third of actual new leases, by the Senate's estimate)."
- **DWE result.** Say "57.6% of voters" or "over a million people". Use 59.1% only as "59.1% of valid votes."
- **Population.** Register: 3,913,644 (2025). Fortschreibung: 3,700,577. The Zensus 2022 correction was ~129k (Zeit) or 124,767 (IBB Tab. 29). Say "about 3.7 million (official count)". For growth use 2011→2025 on the official series (+375k). Don't use BBU's +422k.
- **Asking vs signed rents.** €15.78 (portal median) vs €9.54 (BBU new contracts). Say "*advertised* rents"; footnote that many cheaper flats never reach portals.
- **Vacancy.** 1.97% total, 1.2% for ≥3 months, BBU 1.6%. Never write "below 1%" city-wide.
- **Gap figure.** +119% uses Mietspiegel 2024 (€7.21). With 2026 (€7.71) it is about +105%. Quote IBB's +119% with "(vs Mietspiegel 2024)".
- **Mietspiegel 2023.** Use 7.16, not 7.19. Mark it "index update, set by the Senate alone".
- **Brake breach rates** (45% / 69%) come from the DMB, a tenant lobby, and are based on ads. Say "according to the tenants' federation."
- **Applicant counts.** The ImmoScout 373/day covers top-10% listings only. B.Z.'s "285 per vacant flat" has an unclear method. Single-viewing anecdotes stay anecdotes.
- **GSW per-flat comparison.** Nominal, different portfolios, and the buy-back price includes 450 commercial units. Always give both the €6.2k and €30k figures, or say "including debt."
- **Public stock 1990.** Use 482,000 (Holm). ARCH+ says ~590,000.
- **Vonovia "1% pledge"** was a portfolio average for §558 increases only; end-date sources differ. Avoid precise claims.
- **Mietendeckel duration.** "About 14 months" (23 Feb 2020 → 15 Apr 2021). Stage-2 cuts lasted under 5 months.
- **Döner.** Berlin €7.00 median is crowd-sourced. "Berlin prices fell €0.50–1 in early 2026" (rbb24); do not say "the only city".
- **Bank account needs Anmeldung.** Practice, not law (Basiskonto right). Say "usually."
- **Bürgeramt waits.** 33 days (2024) → 27 days (Sep 2025) → same-day slots (Dec 2025). Date any scarcity claim.
- **Tempelhof vs Monaco.** Monaco's area is quoted from a blog. "About 1.5× Monaco" is safe at any Monaco figure between 2.0 and 2.1 km².
- **Election 2026.** Results are preliminary. Coalition talks began 8 Oct 2026; nothing is decided.
- **Mietrecht II.** Bill numbers changed between drafts. Quote only the cabinet version and say "proposed".
- **Hourly wage €27.42** is a mean. Pair it with the median monthly €4,198, or label it "average".
- **Furniture surcharge.** The current Berlin guideline is ≤2%/month of the furniture's value. Mietrecht II proposes ≤1% or a presumed 10% of rent. Don't mix them up.
