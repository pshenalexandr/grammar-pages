## !!! CORRECTIONS TO THE BRIEF (read first) !!!

1. **The Mietpreisbremse did NOT expire at the end of 2025.**
   - The Bundestag extended it to **31 Dec 2029** on **26 Jun 2025**. The law is dated 17 Jul 2025 (BGBl. 2025 I Nr. 163) and took effect **23 Jul 2025**.
   - Berlin renewed its Verordnung on **11 Nov 2025**. It covers all of Berlin from **1 Jan 2026 to 31 Dec 2029**. Details in section 2.
2. **Mietspiegel 2024 was NOT a Fortschreibung.**
   - The **2023** Mietspiegel was the index-based Fortschreibung. Both tenant and landlord groups walked out, so the Senate published it alone and it was not qualified.
   - **2024** was a newly surveyed **qualified** Mietspiegel, the first one all parties recognised since 2019.
   - **There IS a new qualified Mietspiegel 2026.** It was published 28 May 2026 with a city average of **€7.71/m²**. Details in section 3.
3. **Population: "~3.9M" is wrong for the official figure.**
   - Official population on 31 Dec 2025 is **3,700,577** (Zensus-2022 basis).
   - Census 2022 cut Berlin by about **129,000** people compared with the old projection.
   - "2010 ≈ 3.3M" is roughly right *only on the Zensus-2011 basis*: 3,326,002 in 2011. The pre-2011 projection showed about 3.46M for 2010. Use "≈3.3M (2011) → 3.70M (2025)" and name the basis.
   - The 3.9M figure seems to come from the Melderegister (residents' register), which systematically overcounts. I could not verify a current Melderegister figure.
4. **Vacancy "near 1% or below" needs a qualifier.**
   - Census 2022 (15 May 2022) measured **1.97%** total vacancy (40,681 flats).
   - Only **1.2%** had been empty for ≥3 months.
   - BBU member companies report about 1.6% city-wide, with some districts at 0.8–0.9%.
   - Say "≈2% in the census, ≈1% that is actually available"; do not say "below 1%" flatly.
5. **Renter share "~85%" is fine. Sources state it differently:**
   - Zensus owner-occupier share of occupied flats: **15.8%** (Tagesspiegel) or **16.3%** (wahlatlas), so roughly 84% of occupied flats are rented.
   - BBU states that **82.4% of all flats** (vacant ones included) were rented.
6. **Interest rates:** the brief's "~1% (2021) → 3.5–4% (2023)" is confirmed.
   - Bundesbank, fixation >10 yrs: **1.27% in Sep 2021** → **3.92% in Nov 2023** → **4.04% in Aug 2026**.
   - Rates did not come back down. Aug 2026 is the highest reading since 2023.
7. **New since the brief was written: "Mietrecht II" bill (BT-Drs. 21/6807).**
   - Cabinet approved it 29 Apr 2026; first reading 9 Jul 2026; **still in committee and NOT law** (status as of a 23 Sep 2026 tracker).
   - It would:
     - cap short-term "vorübergehender Gebrauch" lets at 6 (max. 8) months;
     - regulate the furnished-flat surcharge (≤1%/month of the furniture's current value, or a presumed 10% flat rate) and require it to be disclosed;
     - halve index-rent increases above 3% per year in tight markets.
   - The referee draft (Feb 2026) had 3.5% hard cap and 5% furnished flat rate. **These numbers changed** between drafts, so do not quote the Feb version.

## 2. MIETPREISBREMSE (§§ 556d–556g BGB)

### Core rule
- In areas the Land designates by Verordnung, the rent at the start of a tenancy may exceed the ortsübliche Vergleichsmiete (§ 558 Abs. 2) by **at most 10%**.
- A Verordnung **must expire by 31 Dec 2029 at the latest**.
- The four statutory indicators of a tight market are:
  - rents rising much faster than the national average;
  - rent burden much above the national average;
  - population growing without matching new construction;
  - low vacancy with high demand.
- § 556d BGB (current). https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__556d.html

- BVerfG declined a constitutional complaint against it on 24 Jun 2015. https://www.bundesverfassungsgericht.de/SharedDocs/Entscheidungen/DE/2015/06/rk20150624_1bvr136015.html

### Exemptions and loopholes (current law)
- **New builds:** § 556d does not apply to a flat "first used and let after 1 Oct 2014". § 556f S. 1. https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__556f.html
- **First letting after comprehensive modernisation:** both §§ 556d and 556e are disapplied (§ 556f S. 2).
- **Vormiete:** if the previous tenant's last rent was higher than the cap, the landlord may charge up to that Vormiete. Rent reductions, and increases agreed in the last year of the old lease, are ignored (§ 556e Abs. 1). https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__556e.html
- **Modernisation in the 3 years before the lease:** the cap is raised by the amount a § 559 increase would allow (§ 556e Abs. 2).
- **Temporary use:** "Wohnraum, der nur zum vorübergehenden Gebrauch vermietet ist" is excluded from §§ 556d–556g, from rent-increase rules and from eviction protection (§ 549 Abs. 2 Nr. 1).
  - The law currently sets **no time limit**.
  - Student and youth halls are excluded too (§ 549 Abs. 3).
  - https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__549.html
- **Furnished flats:**
  - The brake *does* apply in principle.
  - But no statute sets the furniture surcharge or requires it to be listed separately, so compliance cannot be checked.
  - Hubig on the Mietertag, 27 Jun 2025: "Es kann nicht sein, dass ein Vermieter zwei Stühle in eine leere Wohnung stellt und meint, er könne dann deutlich höhere Preise verlangen." ("It can't be that a landlord puts two chairs in an empty flat and thinks he can then charge much higher prices.") Quoted in BT-Drs. 21/1446. https://dserver.bundestag.de/btd/21/014/2101446.pdf
- **Staffelmiete (§ 557a Abs. 4):** the brake applies to **every step**. Each later step is measured against the Vergleichsmiete on the date it falls due, and a step that was validly reached stays. Rent increases under §§ 558–559b are excluded while the Staffel runs. https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__557a.html
- **Indexmiete (§ 557b Abs. 4):** the brake applies **only to the starting rent**. After that, the rent follows the CPI with no cap; changes at most once a year; § 558 increases are excluded. https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__557b.html
- **Information duty (§ 556g Abs. 1a, from 2019):** a landlord relying on Vormiete, modernisation, the new-build or first-let-after-modernisation exemption must disclose it *before* the contract, in text form. If they don't, they can't rely on it; if they disclose late, they can rely on it only 2 years later. Also: a general duty to provide information on request (Abs. 3). https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__556g.html
- **Repayment (2020 reform):**
  - For contracts from **1 Apr 2020**, a tenant who complains (rügt) **within 30 months** of the tenancy starting can reclaim all overpaid rent back to the start.
  - A later complaint, or one after the tenancy ended, only recovers rent due after the complaint.
  - Before the reform, only rent due after the complaint was recoverable. That gave landlords "economic incentives not to comply", in the government's own words.
  - Gesetz zur Verlängerung und Verbesserung der Regelungen über die zulässige Miethöhe bei Mietbeginn, 19 Mar 2020, BGBl. I S. 540 (published 27 Mar 2020; in force 1 Apr 2020). https://www.bgbl.de/xaver/bgbl/start.xav?start=%2F%2F*%5B%40attr_id%3D%27bgbl120s0540.pdf%27%5D ; BGH materials page: https://www.bundesgerichtshof.de/DE/Bibliothek/GesMat/WP19/M/Miethoehe_Mietbeginn_Verlaengerung_Verbesserung.html
- **No fines:** the BGB contains no fine for breaching the brake. An Expertenkommission is drafting a fine regime and a new § 5 WiStG (Mietwucher) by **31 Dec 2026**. BMJV, 16 Sep 2025. https://www.bmjv.de/SharedDocs/Pressemitteilungen/DE/2025/0916_Mietrecht_Expertenkommission_Auftakt.html
  - Per BT-Drs. 21/7523, the coalition kept "Entfristung" (making the brake permanent) and the new-build cutoff *out* of the commission's remit. https://dserver.bundestag.de/btd/21/075/2107523.pdf

### How often the brake is broken (advertised rents)
- Deutscher Mieterbund / Mietenmonitor, Dec 2025. Based on 16,294 unfurnished pre-2014 listings, 30 May 2024–31 Oct 2025.
  - **>45%** of advertised existing-stock flats in Berlin probably breach the brake.
  - In about one third, the advertised rent exceeds the Mietpreisüberhöhung/Mietwucher thresholds (14.6% / 18.1%).
  - Furnished flats: 21.8% of listings; average €20.54/m² net cold vs €16.37 if the brake were applied; **69% probable breaches**.
  - Advocacy source; check before using. https://mieterbund.de/app/uploads/2025/12/FactSheet_DMB_Mietenmonitor_2025.pdf

  - Tracker status "In der Ausschussberatung" as of 23 Sep 2026. https://bundestagszusammenfasser.de/details?docid=1148
  - The CDU/CSU wants changes friendlier to landlords on short-term lets and Schonfrist. Deubner, update 7 Aug 2026. https://www.deubner-recht.de/themen/neues-mietrecht/mietrechtsreform-2026/
- **Content (government bill version):**
  - **Short-term lets:** "vorübergehender Gebrauch" only for a *special temporary need of the tenant*, **≤6 months**, extendable to **8** if the need grows after the start (new § 549 Abs. 2 Nr. 1).
  - **Furnished:** the Vergleichsmiete may be raised by an "angemessener Möblierungszuschlag", **≤1% per month of the furniture's estimated current value**. For fully furnished flats a surcharge **≤10% of the net cold rent is presumed reasonable** (new § 556d Abs. 1a).
  - The landlord must disclose the surcharge *unprompted* before the contract. If not, the flat counts as **unfurnished** until 2 years after disclosure is made up (new § 556g Abs. 1b).
  - **Indexmiete:** where the Land designates tight areas, the part of annual CPI growth **above 3%** counts **only half** (new § 557b Abs. 4). This also applies to *existing* contracts once the Land's Verordnung is in force.
  - Also: Schonfrist payment can cure an *ordinary* termination for arrears (once per tenancy); the simplified modernisation procedure threshold rises from €10,000 to €20,000; no modernisation increase during a Staffelmiete.
  - **Draft drift:** the Feb 2026 referee draft had a hard **3.5%** index cap and a **5%** furnished flat rate. The cabinet version moved to half-above-3% and 10% (bundesregierung.de, n.d.). https://www.bundesregierung.de/breg-de/aktuelles/besserer-schutz-im-mietrecht-2426570

### Kappungsgrenze (rent increases in existing contracts)
- Up to the Vergleichsmiete, rent may rise by at most 20% in 3 years; **15%** in designated areas (§ 558 Abs. 3).
- Berlin has had 15% city-wide since 19 May 2013.
- Current Verordnung of 14 Mar 2023: **11 May 2023–10 May 2028**. https://www.berlin.de/sen/stadt/_assets/service/rechtsvorschriften/bereich-wohnen/kappungsgrenzenverordnung_2023.pdf ; Senate PR, 14 Mar 2023: https://www.berlin.de/rbmskzl/aktuelles/pressemitteilungen/2023/pressemitteilung.1304009.php

### Modernisierungsumlage (§ 559 BGB)
- **8% of modernisation costs per year** may be added to the annual rent; maintenance share excluded.
- **Caps:** +€3/m²/month within 6 years; +€2/m² if the rent was below €7/m².
- **Heating-system replacements under GEG:** +€0.50/m² within 6 years.
- Hardship clause applies.
- https://www.gesetze-im-internet.de/bgb/__559.html

### Mietendeckel (the 2020 detour)
- Berlin's own rent cap (MietenWoG Bln, 11 Feb 2020) was declared **void in full** by the BVerfG on 25 Mar 2021 (published 15 Apr 2021, 2 BvF 1/20).
- Reason: the Bund regulated rent levels exhaustively in §§ 556–561 BGB, so Berlin had no competence.
- https://www.bundesverfassungsgericht.de/SharedDocs/Pressemitteilungen/DE/2021/bvg21-028.html

## 3. MIETSPIEGEL: how the number is made

- **Legal basis:**
  - §§ 558 Abs. 2, 558c, 558d BGB plus the Mietspiegelverordnung of 28 Oct 2021.
  - The Vergleichsmiete is formed from rents **newly agreed or changed in the last 6 years**. It was **4 years until 31 Dec 2019**: Gesetz zur Verlängerung des Betrachtungszeitraums, 21 Dec 2019, BGBl. I S. 2911, in force 1 Jan 2020. https://www.bgbl.de/xaver/bgbl/start.xav?start=%2F%2F*%5B%40attr_id%3D%27bgbl119s2911.pdf%27%5D
  - A longer window pulls in more old rents, so the Mietspiegel lags the market by design. This matters for the lock-in story.
  - Rents in price-controlled (social) flats, one- and two-family houses and owner-occupied flats are excluded.
- **What's in it (Mietspiegel 2026):**
  - Survey reference date **1 Sep 2025**; about 17,000 data points; **tabular analysis**, not regression.
  - Built by ALP Institut (Hamburg) on a representative primary survey.
  - Recognised by all six associations: Mieterverein, MieterGemeinschaft, Mieterschutzbund, BBU, BFW, Haus & Grund.
  - Covers non-price-controlled flats in buildings with ≥3 flats, ready for occupancy by 31 Dec 2024.
  - **Three tables, one per Wohnlage** (einfach/mittel/gut), split by **Baualter** and **floor area**:
    - Baualter classes: ≤1918, 1919–49, 1950–64, 1965–72, 1973–85 West, 1986–90 West, 1973–90 Ost incl. "Wendewohnungen", 1991–2001, 2002–09, 2010–15, 2016–19, 2020–24.
    - East/West is still split for 1973–90 stock, using pre-2001 district borders.
  - Each cell shows a **median ("Mittelwert") and a ¾-span** (lower/upper). Every cell has ≥30 observations.
  - Flats lacking central heating or a bath get a deduction; for pre-1949 flats it is €0.33/m² (not part of the qualified section).
  - **Orientierungshilfe für die Spanneneinordnung:** a checklist of plus/minus features (bath, kitchen, building, surroundings) that places a flat within the span. It is *not* part of the qualified Mietspiegel, but courts accept it as an estimation basis (BGH VIII ZR 110/04).
  - **Wohnlage** is assigned per address by a statistical model. Indicators: Bodenrichtwert, social-status index, green volume, distance to centres, traffic noise, **crime statistics**, location in a **Milieuschutz area**, distance to U/S/regional rail, green space.
  - Source: Mietspiegel 2026 brochure (May 2026). https://mietspiegel.berlin.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/mietspiegel2026.pdf ; table: https://mietspiegel.berlin.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/mietspiegeltabelle2026.pdf ; method documentation (ALP, Jul 2026): https://mietspiegel.berlin.de/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/260707-Mietspiegeldokumentation-Berlin-2026.pdf ; site: https://mietspiegel.berlin.de/
- **Example cells (2026, mittel/einfach):**
  - Pre-1918 flat under 35 m² in a simple location: €9.58 (span 6.53–13.43).
  - 1950–72 flat ≥85 m², simple location: €6.05.
  - **2020–24 new build under 60 m²: €15.25 (up to 22.70).** The Vergleichsmiete for new builds is close to what Altbau asking rents are.
- **Timeline:**
  - Berlin has issued Mietspiegel since 1987 (ALP documentation); a city-wide one since 2005 (MieterEcho).
  - 2019 qualified (F+B, about 11,000 data points).
  - 2021 index-based (+1.1%), drawn up during the Mietendeckel.
  - 2023 index Fortschreibung (×1.054), **set by the Senate alone after both sides quit**.
  - 2024 newly surveyed, qualified (7.21).
  - **2026 newly surveyed, qualified (7.71; +6.9% vs 2024).**
- Per MieterEcho 2023: from 2005 to 2023 the Mietspiegel average rose €4.49 → €7.16 (+59%), against Berlin median incomes +48% and CPI +39%.
- **Census 2022 comparison:** Berlin's average net cold rent per Zensus (May 2022) was **€7.67/m²**, the cheapest of German cities >600k. München €12.89, Hamburg €9.16. 27.8% of Berlin rentals paid <€6/m². BBU, n.d. (2024). https://bbu.de/beitraege/zensus-2022

## SURPRISING / FUNNY TRUE FACTS (with sources)

1. **In 2001 Berlin planned to tear flats down.** About 100,000 flats stood empty; the Senate planned to demolish up to 7,000 by 2010 and funded the demolition of two high-rises (432 flats) in Marzahn and Lichtenberg. Two decades later its own plan says it needs 222,000 *new* ones. (MieterEcho 292/2001; KA 15/372; StEP 2040)
2. **The 2004 GSW sale price** was about €6,200 per flat before debt (405M / ≈65,700 units). In 2025 an average new-build condo cost **€8,108 per square metre**. (BBU 2004; Gutachterausschuss PR 31 Jul 2026; division is mine)
3. **For three years, more than half of Berlin's rental ads were for furnished temporary flats:** 52% (2022), 57% (2023), 55% (2024). Friedrichshain-Kreuzberg peaked at 72%. (Senate answer 19/26285)
4. **Berliners now stay put for over 22 years on average** in BBU flats, double 2008. Turnover hit a record low of 4.5% in 2025, and B.Z. reports about **285 applicants per vacant flat**. (BBU 14 Sep 2026; B.Z. 17 Jul 2025)
5. **The 2023 Mietspiegel was set by the Senate alone,** after tenant *and* landlord associations walked out; it raised every cell by exactly ×1.054. (Mietspiegel 2023 PDF; MieterEcho 2023)
6. **Crime statistics help set your legal rent:** they are one of the indicators in the Mietspiegel Wohnlage model. (Mietspiegel 2026, section 6.5)
7. **The Justice Minister's "two chairs" line** (Hubig, 27 Jun 2025, quoted in BT-Drs. 21/1446): "a landlord puts two chairs in an empty flat and thinks he can charge much higher prices".
8. **The Mietendeckel lasted about 14 months** (in force Feb 2020) before the BVerfG voided it entirely. It failed because Berlin lacked the power to legislate, not because of its content. (BVerfG PR 28/2021)

## SOURCE DISAGREEMENTS / CAVEATS (summary)

- **Mietspiegel 2023 average:** 7.16 (BBU, Senate) vs 7.19 (MieterEcho lede). Use 7.16.
- **Population growth 2011–2025:** BBU says +422,231 "zensusbereinigt"; the official series gives +374,575 (different 2011 bases).
- **Renter share:** depends on the denominator (occupied flats vs all flats). 82–84%; "~85%" is acceptable rounding.
- **Vacancy:** 1.97% (census, all) vs 1.2% (≥3 months) vs 1.6% (BBU members). "Near 1%" applies only to some districts and to the longer-vacancy measure.
- **Asking rents vs signed rents:**
  - Portal median €15.78 vs BBU new contracts €9.54.
  - Portal data covers only about ⅓ of new leases, and skips LWU/co-op flats that are let offline.
  - The headline "+119%" uses portal medians.
- **Furnished listing counts are inflated** by repeated re-listings (Senate caveat). Some 2020–21 data is flagged as only partly cleaned.
- **Mieterbund breach rates** (45%/69%) are commissioned by an interest group and based on listings, not contracts.
- **Mietrecht II** numbers changed between drafts (3.5% cap → half-above-3%; 5% → 10% furnished flat rate). The bill is not law as of 8 Oct 2026.
